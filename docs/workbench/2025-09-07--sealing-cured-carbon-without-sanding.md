# Sealing Cured Carbon Without Sanding

## Context

The proposed finishing process consisted of applying a final brushed epoxy layer to repair imperfections, optionally adding a vinyl decal, and applying three coats of clear acrylic lacquer. The open question was whether the cured epoxy needed a full `400 -> 600 -> 1000` sanding sequence before the clear coat.

## Decision and outcome

The process was named **Sealing Cured Carbon**.

Sanding was ultimately skipped because it required too much time for insufficient benefit. The clear acrylic lacquer was applied without the full sanding sequence or the proposed spot-sanding workflow.

## Advice considered

The response explained that clear coat can add gloss but does not flatten raised defects, brush marks, runs or dust nibs. A shortened alternative was proposed:

1. Inspect the cured epoxy.
2. Spot-sand raised defects with approximately 400 grit.
3. Optionally make a light 600-grit pass.
4. Remove the sanding residue.
5. Apply two or three clear coats.

This "bump knockdown" process was considered but not adopted.

## Editorial notes

- Hycote clear acrylic lacquer should not be described as clear-coat epoxy spray.
- Sanding to 1000 grit does not itself create a glossy finish; gloss comes from the final coating or subsequent polishing.
- Very fine sanding may provide insufficient mechanical key for some coatings. Surface preparation depends on the selected clear coat's requirements.
- Compatibility and adhesion between the cured epoxy, any vinyl decal and the acrylic lacquer were not tested in the conversation.
- Cleaning with unspecified alcohol was suggested but not adopted.
- No measured comparison between sanded and unsanded finishes was recorded.
