# DNF wetsuit durability and mobility

## Context and requirements

The Cressi Kuwae one-piece smooth-neoprene wetsuit fits tightly and has worked
well for freediving. Its valued qualities are:

- Smooth-skin exterior
- Good, tight fit
- Attractive appearance
- Generally durable materials

However, repeated dynamic no-fins breaststroke movement causes it to tear
around the crotch.

The desired replacement should preserve those qualities while providing more
stretch or a more suitable construction around the crotch. It must remain
appropriate for freediving: conventional breaststroke wetsuit panels that
alter buoyancy are not an acceptable solution.

Current suit reference: Cressi Kuwae, Amazon ASIN `B0BGFH5ZB3`.

## Response received

The first response recommended breaststroke-oriented swimming suits, led by
the Zone3 Active-Flex and Orca Vitalis. That approach was rejected because
their specialised panels and buoyancy distribution were unsuitable for
freediving.

After the clarification, the response changed its selection criteria to thin,
flexible freediving suits with smooth or glide-skin exteriors, minimal seams,
and greater elasticity around the hips and groin. It proposed:

- C4 Sideral 3.5 mm
- Pathos Nerò 2 mm
- SEAC Snake
- Epsealon Abyss 2 mm
- Aqualung Freeflex Pro jacket and trousers
- Fourth Element RF1

The response particularly associated the C4 Sideral and Pathos Nerò with low
panel counts and high elasticity. It also suggested that a thinner suit could
reduce strain during DNF, while acknowledging that thin smooth-skin or
open-cell suits may require more careful handling.

General durability suggestions included easing stress while donning the suit,
inspecting crotch seams and small nicks after sessions, and avoiding sharp
surfaces during use and storage.

## Editorial notes

The second response did not fully respect the clarification: it continued
discussing suits with different panel layouts while redefining the concern as
avoiding only bulky or buoyant panels. It also introduced an unreported £400
budget and 18–22°C water temperature.

Product prices, availability, construction details, and claims that particular
suits would resist crotch tearing were not validated by an actual purchase or
trial. The candidate list therefore remains a set of research leads rather
than recommendations.

No replacement was selected and no subsequent outcome was recorded.
