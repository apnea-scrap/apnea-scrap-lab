# Rebuilding the failed seat of a 2 mm Cressi Ultrastretch suit

## Context

The seat and crotch of a 2 mm Cressi Ultrastretch pool wetsuit repeatedly split as the area stretches.

The suit has:

- an interior fabric matrix
- neoprene foam that remains mostly attached to that fabric
- a smoothskin exterior

Previous repairs using neoprene glue, contact cement, and neoprene repair strips failed. Each repair produced new breaks in the surrounding material.

## Failure hypothesis

The advice received was that the problem is no longer an isolated tear or inadequate adhesive. The entire seat region may have reached its fatigue limit.

Local repairs create relatively stiff areas that cannot elongate with the surrounding suit. Strain is transferred to the edge of each repair, where the weakened neoprene breaks again.

The interior fabric still provides structural continuity, making a resurfacing experiment possible. However, the conclusion that only the exterior smoothskin has failed was an inference rather than something established by inspection.

The seat experiences particularly high strain during:

- DNF frog kicks
- squatting
- pushing off the pool wall
- sitting on the pool edge
- repeated flexion around the central crotch seam

## Preferred repair experiment

Stop chasing individual cracks and overlay the complete seat and crotch with one large sacrificial panel.

Proposed material:

- 1–2 mm neoprene
- smoothskin exterior
- preferably nylon-backed on the bonding side
- sufficiently large to terminate 5–10 cm beyond the damaged region

The patch should be oval, diamond-shaped, or teardrop-shaped, with no sharp corners. It should bridge the central seam and distribute load across approximately 30–40 cm rather than concentrating it around individual cracks.

Proposed stack:

```text
New smoothskin neoprene
Flexible adhesive
Prepared original surface or exposed fabric
Original neoprene
Interior nylon lining
```

Where possible, the new panel should bond to sound fabric or foam rather than loose, cracked smoothskin.

## Proposed surface preparation

1. Remove loose or detached rubber.
2. Trim back delaminated material while preserving the intact interior fabric.
3. Feather crack edges with approximately 180–240 grit abrasive.
4. Scuff glossy smoothskin until the intended bonding area is matt.
5. Remove contamination and allow the area to dry fully.
6. Stretch the suit over a rounded form approximating the shape and strain of the seat when worn.

Possible forming supports include an exercise ball or a bucket packed with towels.

Applying the patch while the seat is under modest pre-stretch was proposed to reduce the additional elongation demanded from the patch during squatting and frog kicking.

## Adhesive proposal

The experimental recommendation was:

- Aquasure FD across the main area for a flexible bond and crack filling
- neoprene cement around the outer 10–15 mm to resist edge peeling

This hybrid adhesive arrangement was proposed during the conversation but has not been tested or adopted.

## Alternative experiments

### Flexible resurfacing

Instead of adding a neoprene panel:

1. Remove loose smoothskin.
2. Abrade the remaining glossy surface.
3. Apply four or five very thin coats of Aquasure FD.
4. Build a continuous flexible membrane approximately 0.2–0.3 mm at a time.

The intended advantage is avoiding patch edges and their associated stress concentrations.

### Fabric-reinforced laminate

A more elaborate proposed laminate was:

```text
New smoothskin
Aquasure FD
Four-way stretch Lycra
Aquasure FD
Original fabric
```

The added fabric would carry tensile load while the new smoothskin restored sealing and abrasion resistance.

### Full panel replacement

The conventional repair would remove the failed seat panel and replace it with fresh neoprene using glued and blind-stitched seams. This is more invasive but avoids bonding a new structure to fatigued material.

### Cast polyurethane skin

A soft Shore 00 polyurethane casting rubber was suggested as an unconventional way to cast a highly extensible replacement skin directly over the fabric. This remained purely speculative.

## Future suit design

For a pool suit used heavily for DNF, consider a large single-piece seat and crotch gusset:

- no central seam in the highest-strain region
- rounded transitions
- a replaceable sacrificial wear layer
- sufficient stretch for deep frog kicks and squatting

## Unresolved questions

- Whether the remaining foam and fabric provide a sound bonding substrate
- Whether the failure is limited to the smoothskin or includes fatigued foam
- Which replacement neoprene is sufficiently extensible
- Whether Aquasure FD works reliably as a large-area laminating adhesive
- Whether the hybrid perimeter-and-centre adhesive arrangement stretches uniformly
- How much pre-stretch should be applied during bonding
- Whether an overlay lasts longer than replacing the complete seat panel
